## The short version

According to the UK Civil Aviation Authority, a technical issue at NATS on 8 September 2026 led to delays and the cancellation of hundreds of UK flights. The CAA considers delays and cancellations directly caused by it likely to count as extraordinary circumstances. So passengers are unlikely to be entitled to compensation for those. Airlines must still look after passengers. The CAA said the same about a second NATS problem on 21 September.

## What the CAA said airlines must still do

According to the CAA, airlines must provide care, offer a refund or re-routing, and promptly reimburse reasonable costs when passengers arrange their own care.

## The 21 September statement

According to the CAA's statement of 21 September 2026, delays and cancellations caused by a technical issue at NATS Prestwick, and some knock-on delays and cancellations, are likely to be extraordinary circumstances. Passengers are therefore unlikely to be entitled to compensation for them. The CAA describes this as illustrative guidance, not a determination in every case.

On care, the CAA says airlines must offer passengers with cancelled flights a refund or re-routing, either at the earliest opportunity or at a later date. While passengers are delayed, airlines must provide meals, refreshments and hotel accommodation in proportion to the delay, with extra help for those who need practical assistance. If an airline cannot offer this care or suitable replacement flights, it should promptly reimburse the reasonable costs of passengers making their own arrangements.

## It is guidance, not a ruling

According to the CAA, this is guidance only. Each case turns on its facts, and passengers can still make a claim, including through the courts.

## What this means for a claim

- The cause matters most. Our article on extraordinary circumstances explains the CAA's categories and its view of technical faults.
- Care while you wait is separate from compensation. Keep itemised receipts for food and hotel costs and claim them from the airline. Our article on food and hotel costs explains how.
- Ask the airline to state the reason it relies on in writing.

## What we could not confirm

We have not seen the outcome of any individual claim after either disruption, so we cannot say how airlines or courts will treat them. We update the rules ledger when the CAA publishes more.
