## The short version

According to GOV.UK, you usually need an electronic travel authorisation (ETA) rather than a visa if you are from Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries. An ETA costs £20 and lets you travel to the UK, Jersey, Guernsey or the Isle of Man for up to 6 months. GOV.UK also says an ETA does not guarantee entry.

## Who needs one

According to GOV.UK:

- Everyone travelling needs their own ETA, including babies and children. You can apply on someone else's behalf.
- You need one if you pass through border control at a UK airport before continuing your journey. If you are unsure, GOV.UK says to check with your airline.
- People with a British or Irish passport do not need an ETA.

GOV.UK's page does not address dual nationals beyond the British and Irish passport case. If you hold another nationality as well, check the official guidance before you book.

## How to apply

According to GOV.UK, you apply through the official application at gov.uk/eta/apply. GOV.UK warns that other websites may charge more, and advises avoiding websites that imitate government services. The fee on the official page is £20.

## What we could not confirm

The GOV.UK pages we read do not state how long an approved ETA stays valid, whether one ETA covers several trips, or how long approval takes. We do not state those figures. Check the application page for them before you book.

## ETIAS is a different thing

The UK ETA is live now. The EU's ETIAS is not. According to the Government of Canada, ETIAS is not yet live, so Canadians do not need one for Europe today. Our guide on whether Canadians need ETIAS has the details.

## Before you travel

Check the official GOV.UK page for the current fee and rules on the day you apply. We change the checked date on this page only after re-reading the sources, and corrections are listed on the corrections page.
